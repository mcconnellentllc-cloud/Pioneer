Just Ask — Real Questions, Instant Answers

Don't know where to start? That's the whole point. You don't need to know anything. Just ask AI like you'd ask a friend. Here are real questions and the kind of answers you get back immediately.

"I want a website for my business but I have no idea where to start. What do I need?"
You need three things, all free: (1) Claude — the AI that writes the code for you at claude.ai, (2) GitHub — where the code gets stored at github.com, and (3) Vercel — the service that puts it on the internet at vercel.com. Sign up for all three with your email. Then come back to me and say "build me a website for [your business name] that does [what you need]" and I'll write the whole thing. You copy it to GitHub, Vercel puts it online, and you have a live website. Total cost: $0.
The old way: Call a web developer, get quoted $2,000–$5,000, wait 3–6 weeks, then realize you can't update it yourself without paying them again.
"How do I build a database? I don't even know what a database is."
A database is just an organized place to store information — think of it like a really powerful spreadsheet that your website can read from and write to. The easiest way to build one is Airtable (airtable.com). It looks and feels exactly like a spreadsheet, but it's a real database underneath. Sign up for free, click "Create a base," and describe what you want to track. Airtable even has a built-in AI assistant — you can tell it "I want to track my customers, their orders, and what they owe me" and it builds the whole database for you. No coding. No commands. Just plain English.
The old way: Hire a developer to set up a database you can't see, can't touch, and can't update without calling them.
"What kind of stuff can I store in a database?"
Anything you'd put in a spreadsheet or a filing cabinet — and a lot more. Here are real examples: Customer lists (names, phone numbers, addresses, order history, notes). Inventory (parts on hand, quantities, prices, reorder points). Equipment records (make, model, hours, service dates, costs). Field data (crop planted, inputs applied, yields, revenue per acre). Invoices and billing (who owes what, due dates, payment status). Grain contracts (bushels, prices, delivery dates, elevator). Employee hours. Maintenance logs. Anything you currently track on paper, in your head, or in a messy Excel file — a database does it better, and you can access it from your phone.
The old way: Keep it in your head, scribble it on a notepad, or dig through a shoebox of receipts.
"I don't want people to see my data. How do I keep it private?"
Your data is private by default on all of these tools. Airtable: your bases are only visible to you unless you specifically invite someone. Nobody can find them or search for them. GitHub: you can make your code repository private (free) so only you can see it. Vercel: you can add password protection to your site, or keep certain pages behind a login. If you're storing sensitive information (financials, customer data, contracts), keep your GitHub repo set to "Private" and use Airtable's built-in permissions to control who sees what. Your data is yours. Nobody sees it unless you let them.
The old way: Worry about it, do nothing, and keep everything on paper because "at least nobody can hack a filing cabinet."
"Can AI really build me a whole website? Like a real one?"
Yes. This website you're looking at right now — YourAgInfo.com — was built entirely by AI. Every page, every section, every line of code. It's not a template from Wix or Squarespace. It's a custom-built site that does exactly what we wanted. And you can do the same thing. Tell Claude "build me a website for my seed dealership with a product catalog, a contact page, and a page about our team" and it writes the whole thing in a few minutes. It's a real website with real code that you own and control — not a rental from a website builder that charges you $30/month forever.
The old way: Pay Wix or Squarespace $30/month for a template site you don't own, or pay a developer thousands for a custom one.
"I'm not technical at all. I barely use my phone. Can I still do this?"
If you can send a text message, you can do this. That's literally all it is — texting. You open Claude on your phone, type (or speak) what you want, and Claude does the technical part. You don't need to know what HTML is. You don't need to understand code. You don't need to watch a YouTube tutorial. Just describe what you want like you're talking to a person: "I want a page that shows my equipment for sale with photos and prices." Claude handles everything else. Start small — ask Claude to build you something simple and see what happens. You'll be surprised.
The old way: Assume technology isn't for you and miss out, or pay someone else to do what you could do yourself in 10 minutes.

Tips for Talking to AI

Be Specific

Instead of "help with marketing," say "write a Facebook post about our Pioneer seed plot day on June 15 in Haxtun." The more details, the better the answer.

Give Context

Tell the AI who you are: "I'm a farmer in NE Colorado with 2,000 dryland acres." This helps it give you relevant, local answers.

Ask Follow-Ups

Don't settle for the first answer. Say "make it shorter," "add pricing," or "now do one for soybeans." The conversation keeps going.

Double-Check Facts

AI is a great starting point, but always verify important numbers, dates, and claims — especially for chemical rates, regulations, or legal matters.
