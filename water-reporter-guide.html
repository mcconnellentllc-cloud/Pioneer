1. What is Water Reporter?

Water Reporter is Corteva's irrigation decision tool. It combines satellite imagery, weather data, pivot telemetry, and crop models to give you a daily picture of soil moisture across every field.

Instead of guessing when to run your pivots, Water Reporter shows you:

  • How much water is in the soil right now
  • How fast the crop is using it (daily ET)
  • When you'll hit stress levels if you don't irrigate
  • How much to apply and where in the field needs it most
  • Whether rain is coming that could delay your next set
Free for Pioneer customers. Water Reporter is available at no additional cost through your Pioneer rep. It works with Valley/AgSense and Lindsay/FieldNET pivot systems.

5. What the Numbers Mean

Water Reporter shows several key metrics. Here's what each one means and why it matters for your irrigation decisions:

Plant Available Water (PAW)

MOST IMPORTANT
What it is: The amount of water in the soil that the crop can actually access, expressed as a percentage (0-100%) or in inches.

What it means:
• 100% PAW = Field capacity. Soil is holding all the water it can. No irrigation needed.
• 50-100% = Adequate. Crop has plenty of water. Monitor but no action needed.
• 30-50% = Getting dry. Start planning your next irrigation set.
• Below 30% = Stress zone. Crop is pulling hard. Irrigate soon.
• Below 15% = Yield loss happening. Water immediately.

For corn: Keep PAW above 50% during tasseling and pollination (VT-R1). This is when corn uses 0.30-0.35 inches per day. Dropping below 50% PAW during pollination can cost 5-8% yield per day of stress.

Evapotranspiration (ET)

DAILY WATER USE
What it is: How much water the crop and soil lose each day through evaporation and plant transpiration, measured in inches per day.

Typical corn ET rates by growth stage:
• Emergence (V1-V4): 0.05-0.10 in/day
• Vegetative (V6-V12): 0.15-0.25 in/day
• Late vegetative (V14-VT): 0.25-0.30 in/day
• Tasseling/Pollination (VT-R1): 0.30-0.35 in/day (peak demand)
• Grain fill (R2-R4): 0.25-0.30 in/day
• Late grain fill (R5-R6): 0.15-0.20 in/day
• Maturity: 0.05-0.10 in/day

How to use it: ET tells you how fast the tank is draining. If ET is 0.30 in/day and you applied 1 inch, that application lasts about 3 days before you're back where you started. On hot, windy days ET can spike to 0.40+ in/day.

Soil Water Deficit

INCHES NEEDED
What it is: How many inches of water you'd need to bring the soil back to field capacity.

Example: If your deficit shows 2.5 inches and your pivot applies 0.75 inches per pass, you need about 3 passes to fill back up. But you don't always need to get back to 100% — getting back to 60-70% PAW is usually fine during mid-season.

Rule of thumb: Don't let the deficit exceed 3 inches during reproductive stages. You can't catch up once you fall behind during pollination.

Growing Degree Units (GDUs)

CROP STAGING
What it is: A measure of heat accumulation that drives crop development. Calculated daily as: (Daily High + Daily Low) / 2 - 50, with the high capped at 86°F and the low floored at 50°F.

Why it matters: Water Reporter uses GDUs to estimate what growth stage your corn is in, which determines the crop coefficient used to calculate ET. A corn plant at VT uses water completely differently than one at V6.

Key corn GDU milestones:
• Emergence: ~120 GDUs
• V6 (6-leaf): ~475 GDUs
• V12 (12-leaf): ~870 GDUs
• VT (tassel): ~1,135 GDUs
• R1 (silking): ~1,200 GDUs
• R6 (black layer): ~2,700 GDUs

Full-season hybrids (108 day) need approximately 2,700-2,800 GDUs to reach maturity. NE Colorado typically accumulates 2,650-2,740 GDUs per season.

Effective Precipitation

RAIN THAT COUNTS
What it is: Not all rainfall reaches the root zone. Light showers may evaporate before soaking in. Heavy downpours may run off. Water Reporter calculates the amount of rain that actually enters the soil and becomes available to the crop.

General rule:
• Rain events under 0.10 inches — mostly evaporates, minimal benefit
• 0.10 - 0.50 inches — partially effective (50-70% reaches roots)
• 0.50 - 1.00 inches — mostly effective (70-90%)
• Over 1.00 inches — some runoff likely, effectiveness drops

Water Reporter adjusts for this automatically, but if you notice your soil moisture didn't respond to a rain event you logged, this is probably why.

Water Use Efficiency (WUE)

SEASON METRIC
What it is: Bushels of grain produced per inch of total water (irrigation + effective precipitation). Measured at the end of the season.

Corn benchmark: Good water use efficiency for irrigated corn in NE Colorado is 8-10 bushels per inch. If you're making 200 bu/acre with 22 total inches of water, your WUE is about 9.1 bu/inch — that's solid.

Watch for variability within a field. If one corner shows yellow while the rest is green, that area has different soil (sandier, shallower topsoil, or a knob). Water Reporter helps you see where your pivot is under-applying. Consider adjusting nozzle packages or speed in those zones.

How the map updates: Satellite imagery refreshes every 1-3 days depending on cloud cover. Weather data and ET calculations update daily. If it's been cloudy, the map may show the last clear-sky reading with modeled estimates for the days since.

7. How to Add Rainfall

Water Reporter uses weather station data for rainfall, but local rain can vary significantly from what the nearest station recorded. You can manually enter rainfall for more accurate soil moisture tracking:

  1. Open the field you want to update
  2. Look for the rainfall/precipitation input area (usually near the irrigation log or weather panel)
  3. Select the date the rain occurred
  4. Enter the amount in inches — use your rain gauge, not the weather forecast. If you measured 0.65 inches at your home gauge, enter 0.65
  5. Save — Water Reporter will recalculate soil moisture using your actual reading
Pro Tip: Keep a rain gauge near each pivot or at least one per farm. Thunderstorm rain can vary by an inch or more across a quarter section. The closest weather station may be 10+ miles away and completely missed your storm.
Don't skip light rains. Even 0.15-0.20 inches helps reset the surface and reduces the next day's evaporation. It may not show on the map, but it adds up over a week. Enter everything you measure.
After heavy rain (1"+): Wait 24 hours before checking the map. Water Reporter needs time to account for drainage and runoff. The soil moisture reading the day after a big rain may overstate what's actually available to the crop until excess water drains below the root zone.

8. Irrigation Tracking

If your pivot telemetry is connected (AgSense or FieldNET), Water Reporter automatically records:

  • When the pivot started and stopped
  • How far around the circle it ran (degrees)
  • Estimated application amount (based on speed and nozzle package)
  • Time to complete the circle at current speed

This data feeds directly into the soil moisture model. You don't need to log irrigation manually if telemetry is working.

Verify your application rate. If Water Reporter thinks your pivot applies 0.75 inches per pass but you know it's actually closer to 0.60, the soil moisture estimates will drift. Check your pivot's nozzle chart against what Water Reporter shows. Tell your rep if there's a mismatch so the model can be calibrated.
Manual irrigation entry: If you irrigate from a well or flood and don't have telemetry, you'll need to manually log each irrigation event (date, field, and inches applied). Otherwise Water Reporter won't know about the water you put on.

9. Daily Workflow

Here's how to use Water Reporter as part of your daily routine:

  1. Morning check (5 min) — Open Water Reporter and scan the map. Look for any fields turning yellow or orange. These need attention today or tomorrow
  2. Check the forecast — Is rain expected? If there's a 60%+ chance of 0.50+ inches in the next 48 hours, you might delay a set. But don't gamble during pollination — irrigate anyway
  3. Review ET — Note today's ET rate. If it's above 0.30 in/day and climbing, your pivots need to be running. A pivot applying 0.75 in/pass at 0.30 daily ET only buys you 2.5 days
  4. Plan irrigation sets — Prioritize fields with the lowest PAW, especially those in reproductive stages (VT through R4). A corn field at VT with 35% PAW is more urgent than a V8 field at 40% PAW
  5. Log any manual rainfall — If it rained overnight at specific fields, enter gauge readings. This keeps the model accurate
  6. End of week — Compare the week's irrigation applied vs. ET. Are you keeping up? Are you falling behind on any fields? Adjust your schedule for next week
2026 Drought Season: With Phillips, Sedgwick, and Yuma Counties in Extreme Drought (D3) and precipitation running at half of normal, every irrigation decision matters. Water Reporter is especially valuable this year — don't waste a pass on a field that doesn't need it when another field is at 25% PAW.

10. Troubleshooting

"My irrigation isn't showing up"

Check your AgSense/FieldNET connection. In AgSense 365, go to Settings → API Partners and make sure "Granular Ag" is checked. Data syncs overnight, so irrigation from today won't appear until tomorrow. If it still doesn't show after 48 hours, contact your Pioneer rep.

"The soil moisture doesn't match what I see in the field"

This usually means the model needs calibration. Common causes: incorrect application rate for your nozzle package, unlogged rainfall, or the soil type in the model doesn't match your field. Report the discrepancy to your rep so they can adjust the model inputs.

"The map hasn't updated in days"

Satellite imagery depends on clear skies. During extended cloudy periods, the map may show the last clear-sky observation. ET calculations and weather data still update daily, so the soil moisture numbers and recommendations are still current even if the visual map is a few days old.

"I can't log in"

Water Reporter is at wreporter.pd.research.corteva.com. If your credentials don't work, contact your Pioneer rep or Corteva Digital Ag Support at (800) 233-7333 or digitalagsupport@corteva.com.

"A field is missing from my dashboard"

Field boundaries come from Granular Insights. Make sure the field exists in your Granular account with accurate boundaries. If it's there but not in Water Reporter, your rep needs to enroll it through Corteva's field registration tool.
