Conditions

Weather × Yield Correlation

How did air temperature, soil temperature, and soil moisture during the planting window affect yield? Daily air temp estimates from NWS regional summaries; soil temp at 4″ modeled from air temp using exponential smoothing. Topsoil moisture from USDA NASS weekly crop progress reports.

Irrigated: Planting Conditions vs. Yield

April 15 – May 15. Two hard freezes (Apr 18, May 1) dropped soil temps below 50°F. The peak yield window (Apr 18–29) coincided with soil temps in the 46–50°F range — seeds waited in cool soil, then emerged into warming conditions.

Dryland: Planting Conditions vs. Yield

May 1 – June 3. Soil temps crossed the 50°F planting threshold around May 8. Best dryland yields came from early May planting (May 1–12) as May 5–6 storms broke the drought. After May 12, warm soil couldn’t offset lost growing season days.

Key Takeaways

1. Soil temp ≠ yield predictor. Irrigated peak yields came when soil was 46–50°F — below the 50°F threshold. Cool soil delayed emergence but didn’t hurt final yield. Getting seed in the ground early mattered more than waiting for warm soil.

2. Freeze events marked boundaries, not barriers. The Apr 18 hard freeze (22°F low) and May 1 freeze (25°F low) bookend the irrigated peak window. Corn planted just before or during these events still yielded 240–270 bu/ac.

3. Moisture trumped temperature for dryland. With topsoil 92% short-to-very-short through April, the May 5–6 rain event was the inflection point. Dryland corn planted around that moisture pulse outperformed later plantings into warm, dry soil.

4. Late planting penalty is about GDUs, not conditions. By mid-May, soil was 58–62°F and moisture had improved — ideal germination conditions. But yields cratered because the growing season was too short to accumulate enough heat units.

About This Data

Aggregated from area Pioneer data across Logan, Phillips, and Sedgwick counties. 2026 crop year, Pioneer brand only.

Yield and seeding rate are as-reported from combine and planter data. Dryland entries require a minimum of 10 harvest acres; irrigated entries require 15 acres. Entries below these thresholds are shown with reduced emphasis.

This data reflects actual grower results from a wide range of field conditions, soil types, and management practices. It is not a controlled experiment. Use alongside test plot data and multi-year performance records for product positioning decisions.

Weather data sources: Daily air temperatures estimated from NWS Boulder and Goodland regional summaries, BoulderCast weekly reviews, HPRCC climate reports, and Haxtun climate normals. Soil temperature at 4″ depth modeled using exponential smoothing of daily mean air temperature (not station observations). Topsoil moisture ratings from USDA NASS Colorado Crop Progress reports. These are estimates for the Haxtun, CO area — not station-recorded observations.
